# Concept 04 visual synthesis and revised creative brief

27 October 2026. Codex and Claude Code independently inspected the exact softened Concept 04 PNG, then completed separate reviews in BB. **Keep the editorial foundation, improve how the product explains and supports a small caregiver adjustment, and test a simpler icon.** The board warrants further prototyping; neither review establishes that it is the strongest possible direction.

The [Codex report](codex-visual-review.md), [Claude Code report](claude-code-visual-review.md), [original board](assets/onda-concept-04-wonder-into-practice.png), [asset provenance](assets/source-manifest.json), [inspection receipts](image-inspection-receipts.json) and [review provenance](provenance.json) are preserved. Earlier text-only reports and [provisional synthesis](synthesis.md) remain historical, superseded for visual assessment by these reports.

## What the pixels support

The board combines an ivory/aubergine editorial serif, apricot buttons with dark text, lilac/chartreuse collage, a caregiver and child in a mirrored attentive pose, and three phones labeled Notice, Understand and Try. The Try phone contains a smaller “Later / Reflect” card. The example progresses from “We were late. I rushed. My child hid their shoes” to “Leaving a good moment can be hard,” then a two-minute-warning suggestion.

Both critics found the identity cohesive, warm and adult-oriented. They favor retaining the contrast between expressive surrounding artwork and relatively simple task content, the tentative question “What else could be true?”, and one prominent action per screen. Those are design judgments grounded in visible elements, not evidence of broad parent preference or effectiveness.

## Three highest-value next changes

1. **Make the interpretation earned and correctable — high priority.** Label the highlighted explanation as a possibility, ask for missing context when it matters, and provide a visible “doesn't fit”/edit path. Keep observed events separate from interpretation. Test whether caregivers can reject a plausible but wrong explanation and recover. Tradeoff: extra questions or alternative cards add effort; use progressive disclosure rather than a diagnostic questionnaire.
2. **Make one feasible adult adjustment the immediate outcome — high priority.** Carry forward the parent's own context (“I rushed”), allow a smaller step or “not now,” and offer optional private rehearsal. Place reflection after an attempt or clearly tie it to an earlier one. Compare a compact moment-first route with a guided learning route. Tradeoff: a shortcut can become generic advice; preserve a concise reason and a way to revisit it. Do not turn five conceptual stages into five compulsory screens.
3. **Refine a scalable, legible identity — medium priority before system adoption.** Retain ivory/aubergine, dark-on-apricot controls and editorial warmth. Simplify the brush-loop mark for 16/32 px and monochrome use; reduce redundant slogans; define violet and pastel roles; trim decorative material where longer content needs room. Tradeoff: excessive simplification could erase the playful character Miguel likes. Keep texture and collage expressive at welcome/editorial moments, restrained around active decisions.

## Contrast and measurement limits

Codex inspected the image but did not measure contrast after finding Pillow unavailable. Claude used an existing standard-library PNG decoder and region samples: dark button text on apricot was approximately 8.0–8.2:1; the first terracotta step numeral against the screen approximately 3.3:1; sampled pastel swatches against board ivory approximately 1.5–2.3:1. Its [measurement receipts](contrast-measurement-receipts.json) preserve the scripts and outputs.

These are sampled raster ratios, not source color tokens, worst-case texture measurements, font-size determinations or compliance certification. The darkest-pixel method can overstate thin-stroke visibility; nearby-background samples can miss actual adjacent texture. Pastel decorative swatches are not automatically text failures. Verify real component colors, text sizes, focus/disabled states and device rendering during implementation. Claude's estimate of physical device type size from this marketing-board scale is not reliable enough to adopt; source points and actual phone layouts remain unknown.

The reports' language about “common wellness tropes,” losing an earlier orange “edge,” or absent product capabilities should also be bounded. Neither received the original neon board for comparison or conducted category-wide visual research. A missing affordance on a three-phone concept does not prove the eventual product lacks it. Claude's statement that the name is “not final” is imprecise: Onda is the user's chosen working name, while legal clearance remains outstanding.

## Revised creative brief

Develop **Wonder into practice** as an expressive adult parenting identity: curiosity should lead to a small, feasible change in how the caregiver responds. Keep the ivory/aubergine editorial foundation, softened apricot, lilac/chartreuse support, textured illustration and human warmth. Rich visuals should carry meaning around a clear utility layer.

Tell one specific episode honestly. Show the caregiver's account, a conditional alternative reading, a visible correction, one chosen adjustment and optional words to rehearse. Let reflection accept “not tried,” “mixed,” “made things worse” and continued child distress without grading either person. Include shared joy and repair in the wider system, not only difficult transitions. Parent-private context and remembering/sharing controls need their own designed states before real use.

Use one primary tagline, an icon that works without texture or color, and short display headlines with a readable sans utility layer. Extend illustration across ordinary, imperfect situations and varied caregivers without claiming universal appeal. Retain Onda as the working name; this work does not establish trademark clearance, diagnostic authority or relationship outcomes.

Two testable alternatives within Miguel's taste:

- **Moment first:** “One moment, one possibility, one thing to try.” A compact task card in a rich editorial frame, with the deeper loop available on demand. This follows Codex's urgency emphasis.
- **Field notes on a wavelength:** A richer explanatory spread shows the entire learning loop, including private rehearsal and honest reflection. This follows Claude's learning emphasis. Use it to explain the product without imposing the whole spread as mandatory task flow.

Compare both against the current Concept 04 using the same episode, content quality and visual effort. This is a revised brief, not new artwork or a final direction selection.

## Proposed real-parent tests

- Test first-impression meaning: understanding and parent adaptation, generic advice, diagnosis or evaluation? Ask before explaining the concept.
- Give a plausible wrong interpretation. Observe whether participants identify it as a guess, correct it, and recover without feeling blamed.
- Compare guided and compact routes on real small phones with longer content, larger text and mild distraction. Observe next-action discovery, burden, skip/rehearsal choice and recovery after interruption.
- Ask what the caregiver would change about their own response, and whether that change is feasible given limited capacity. Test an unsuccessful attempt and optional later reflection.
- Probe self-recognition and cultural fit with diverse family/caregiver contexts and English/Spanish participants. Illustration identity and visual preference do not substitute for product relevance.
- Test monochrome/16/32 px icon recognition and delayed recall, and compare the two proposed presentations with an equivalent general-assistant response.

Specify recruitment, consent, success/failure criteria and the scope of claims before testing. No real-parent study, accessibility audit, efficacy study, revised artwork, deployment or outreach was performed here.
