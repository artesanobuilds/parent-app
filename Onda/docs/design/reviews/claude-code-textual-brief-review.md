## 1. Does the concept fit the brief? (text only)

**Strong fit, at least on paper**
- **[S]→[R]** The Notice → Understand → Try → Reflect flow matches the product loop in [R]: notice an episode, reinterpret it with curiosity, pick one adjustment, rehearse, try, reflect. The four labels make the loop easy to teach. That is the concept's biggest product asset.
- **[S]** "Rich visuals around clean utility" is the right idea for "fun but professional editorial." Expression stays in the margins and the task stays calm.
- **[S]** Showing an adult caregiver, not a child, fits "change how *I* show up." It avoids putting the child under scrutiny.

**Gaps against the brief**
1. **Rehearse is missing from the flow.** [R] lists "rehearse privately" as its own step. The supplied flow jumps from Understand to Try. **[I]** That drops something the brief treats as core, and it could make Try feel like a prescription instead of something the parent practiced and chose. **[H]** Check whether any phone screen shows rehearsal.
2. **Observation vs. interpretation vs. uncertainty.** [R] wants observation, the adult's reading, the child's possible view and uncertainty kept separate, with corrections visible. A three-phone hero flow usually shows the happy path. **[H]** Check that the Understand screen labels these layers, for example "What you saw / How you read it / What it might be like for them / We're not sure about." Check for a visible way to correct it. If Understand reads as one confident explanation, the board breaks the no-diagnosis spirit even with no score on screen.
3. **"Ask before remembering," privacy and sharing.** None of this appears in the supplied description. **[I]** A static board may skip it, but then the board can't show the trust promise. That promise may be the real difference from a "competent general assistant," which [R] says must be beaten.
4. **Low capacity.** [R] says low capacity should make the next action smaller. **[H]** Check whether Try ever offers a "smaller step" or "not now" option, or only one cheerful action card.
5. **Joy and repair.** [R] says joy matters alongside difficulty, and repair shouldn't demand forgiveness. **[H]** If every example moment is a meltdown, the board turns Onda into a crisis tool. If the Reflect screen implies success ("It worked! 🎉"), it brings in the scoring logic the brief forbids.
6. **Reflect can turn into a score.** **[H]** Streaks, ticks, progress rings, "3 of 5," mood faces or any before/after visual are hidden scores. Reflect copy should accept "it didn't land" and "my child is still upset" as normal outcomes, not failures.

## 3. Distinctiveness (my first guesses)

- **[I]** Serif wordmark, ivory/aubergine, collage, hand-drawn ink: this is now a common 2024–26 "warm editorial wellness" style (Headspace-adjacent, Calm editorial, many DTC therapy brands). The softened palette likely cost some of the edge the neon orange gave. Pastel apricot, lilac and chartreuse risks blending in.
- **[H]** What could set it apart is the curiosity-loop icon and the specific Notice/Understand/Try/Reflect language. A generic loop or spiral would undo that. Check whether the icon reads as "wave/onda," "question" or "loop," and whether it holds up at 16–29 px.
- **[I]** A serif wordmark plus textured collage may break down at app-icon size, in notifications and in dark mode. Test the icon alone, without texture.

## 5. Parent tests to plan now (format-independent)

- **Five-second / first-click test** on the board. Can parents say what Onda does in one sentence, and do they describe it as help with understanding or as an evaluation?
- **Layer comprehension.** On the Understand screen, can parents point to which statement is their observation and which is a guess about the child? This mirrors the [R] next-evidence check.
- **Hidden-score probe.** Show the Reflect screen and ask, "How did this parent do?" If people answer in grades, there is an implied score.
- **Stress or low-capacity proxy.** Arm's-length phone, one hand, a timed glance after a distraction task. Can they find the next small step?
- **Self-recognition across a range of caregivers.** Include fathers, grandparents, a second-language Spanish-speaking parent and a low-income caregiver. Ask "Is this for someone like you?" Don't assume broad appeal.
- **Comparison.** The same moment handled by a general assistant (a plain chat). Does Onda's visual frame add trust or clarity, or is it just decoration?
