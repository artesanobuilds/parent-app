# Parent app iteration 2: parent adaptation, relationship quality, and the alliance analogy

Research date: 13 October 2026. This is a targeted evidence review and product-research proposal, not a systematic review, clinical assessment, or validated intervention. No real family was assessed and no child data were transmitted.

## Bottom line

The reframing has a credible developmental-science foundation: help a parent notice this child's cues, reconsider their own interpretation, and adjust their timing, language, expectations, environment, or response. The defensible unit of change is the parent's behavior in a specific interaction, with the child's perspective and experience respected. It is not changing a child's personality or maximizing compliance.

The therapeutic-alliance analogy can supply questions about feeling understood, voice, shared understanding, and repairing misunderstandings. It does not establish that a universal **parent–child alliance score** exists, nor that a therapy questionnaire can be relabeled for families. In this search, I did not find validation of a general-purpose, cross-age consumer-app composite with that intended interpretation. That is a bounded negative finding, not proof that no related measure exists.

Recommendation: launch a multidimensional, non-diagnostic reflection and parent-adjustment flow without a composite relationship grade. Keep research outcome measures separate from everyday UX. Consider any future score a new measurement-development project, requiring evidence of both interpretability and benefit over the no-score version.

## 1. What the science supports, and what it does not

### Goodness of fit and temperament

Goodness of fit concerns how expectations and environmental demands meet a child's characteristics. It does not mean that parent and child must have matching personalities. A longitudinal study of 153 early adolescents related poor fit between temperament and parent/peer demands to poorer adjustment. Its observational design cannot tell an app which strategy will causally help a particular child. [Primary study, Lerner et al., 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0140-1971(90)90019-4)

Temperament-informed intervention is not purely theoretical: INSIGHTS trials tested a multicomponent program involving parents, teachers, and children. They do not isolate an algorithmic matching rule, a parent-only app, or the incremental benefit of collecting a detailed child profile. Product personalization must earn its added data burden against a simpler alternative. [Primary INSIGHTS trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2830583/)

An especially important boundary comes from Rothbart's own research group: its questionnaires were developed for research samples, are not recommended for individual child assessment, and have no universal comparison norms. The authors say that completing them may encourage parental reflection but should not be treated as clear evidence of an individual child's temperament. Age and culture matter. Research use is available without charge; this does not establish commercial-app rights. [Instrument-owner FAQ](https://research.bowdoin.edu/rothbart-temperament-questionnaires/frequently-asked-questions/)

**Product inference:** use context-specific, revisable observations such as “Transitions have been harder after busy afternoons,” not fixed types such as “Your child is resistant.” The parent can change the setup without making the child defective. A necessary boundary can remain while timing, explanation, support, and available choices change.

### Reflective functioning and mentalization

The relevant capability is curiosity about the child's experience together with recognition that the parent can be mistaken. PRFQ development research distinguishes prementalizing, certainty about mental states, and interest/curiosity. It explicitly discusses intrusive overcertainty and overelaborate interpretations. The original studies were preliminary, with important self-report and generalizability limitations; “more certainty” is not a universal improvement. [Primary PRFQ validation, 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0176218)

A 2025 Rasch analysis in 531 Danish mothers at risk of depression, with infants aged 2–11 months, found problems distinguishing all seven response categories and recommended curvilinear interpretation for certainty and curiosity. This is direct evidence against a simple “more mentalizing points = better parent” design, not a reason to introduce the study's recoding as a universal app rule. [Primary PRFQ-I validation, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39887830/)

**Product inference:** distinguish observation, interpretation, alternatives, and what the child actually said. Use “One possibility is…” and “What would help us check?” The AI should help the parent stay open to correction, not supply purported privileged access to the child's mind.

### Responsiveness and autonomy support

Parent behavior is a plausible intervention target. A randomized video-feedback trial in families with children under two found short-term improvement in observed interaction relative to usual care. It involved structured human-delivered support and observation, not a chatbot. [Primary VIPI trial, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25699090/)

Autonomy support concerns supporting volition and perspective-taking. It is distinct from simply promoting independence, and it can coexist with structure. A primary study explicitly separated promotion of independence from promotion of volitional functioning. [Soenens et al., 2007](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2007_SoenensVansteenkisteLens_DP.pdf)

In an RCT of 293 parent–child dyads, the How-to Parenting Program improved parent-reported autonomy support, with more qualified affiliation/structure findings and limited child-reported differences. This supports investigating parent change and also shows why parent satisfaction or self-report alone cannot substantiate improved child experience. [Primary RCT, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appdev.2021.101383)

**Product inference:** optimize for a parent offering a clear reason, acknowledging a feeling, and allowing real bounded choices. Do not treat a child's disagreement, need for accommodation, slower response, or choice not to talk as failure. Respectful limits are compatible with listening.

### Rupture and repair

A prospective study of 96 mothers and three-year-olds found that greater observed repair during a challenging puzzle task predicted better regulatory outcomes four months later. Repair was operationalized in a specific coded interaction; the study does not justify a universal repair ratio or prescribe how fast a child must reconnect. [Primary study, “Can We Fix This?”, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28190911/)

**Product inference:** help the adult return, acknowledge their part, and invite reconnection without requiring forgiveness, affection, or reassuring the parent. Avoid perfect-attunement promises, repair streaks, and claims that one difficult interaction damages attachment.

## 2. Keep the constructs separate

| Construct | What it concerns | What an app must not silently substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Therapy working alliance | Collaboration in a helping/treatment relationship: bond, engagement with tasks, and agreement about goals | The whole lifelong parent–child relationship or a parental right to set the child's goals |
| Parent–child relationship quality | Multiple experiences such as warmth, conflict, communication, acceptance | A single rating of whether either person is good |
| Attachment security | How a specific relationship functions as a source of security, especially under distress and exploration | Current mood, enjoyment, cooperation, a social-media attachment type, or attachment-disorder diagnosis |
| Reflective functioning | Parent's capacity to consider minds, including uncertainty about interpretations | Accurate mind-reading or a direct measure of the child's internal state |
| Autonomy support | Respect for the child's perspective and volition | Unlimited choice, permissiveness, or absence of necessary boundaries |
| Goodness of fit | Contextual match between demands/support and characteristics | Trait similarity, a compatibility destiny, or child compliance |
| Satisfaction | One person's evaluative feeling about the relationship or app | Demonstrated responsiveness or the other person's experience |
| Parenting alliance | In PAM/PAI terminology, cooperation between co-parents | Parent–child alliance |

The WAI creator describes alliance as an aspect of a helping relationship, not everything in that relationship. This is a useful conceptual analogy but requires new evidence when roles, goals, power, developmental capacities, and context change. [WAI creator's construct description](https://wai.profhorvath.com/what-is)

Attachment assessment has age-specific methods and requires relevant skills. NICE's guidance concerns children in or near care; it should not be generalized into consumer screening. It also distinguishes assessment of difficulties from diagnosis, which requires diagnostic criteria. [NICE NG26](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG26/chapter/recommendations)

Even the adolescent IPPA is not interchangeable with attachment organization: a primary adolescent study explicitly makes that distinction. [Allen et al., primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/9839424/)

## 3. Instrument shortlist and practical constraints

“Validated” describes support for a particular interpretation in specified populations and contexts. It is not a permanent property that transfers automatically to any app, language, age, respondent, or purpose.

### CPRS / CPRS short form: closest straightforward parent-report relationship candidate

- The 15-item form measures parent-perceived **closeness and conflict separately**, for children ages 3–12 according to the owner. It does not include the child's direct perspective.
- Driscoll and Pianta's 2011 study included 563 two-parent families; children of color were 7% of the sample. Conflict alpha ranged .78–.84, closeness .64–.74. Correlations with observed parenting were generally small. These are useful cautions against individual precision, caregiver ranking, and universal norms.
- The study examined development across years, not reliable daily app change. I did not establish an app-specific minimal important change or reliable-change threshold.
- UVA permits educational-research use and publication; commercial digital deployment is not explicitly authorized. The page's general no-wording-change instruction conflicts with a CPRS-specific allowance for cultural/contextual wording changes. Seek written clarification before commercial use or adaptation.

[Owner, age and permissions](https://education.virginia.edu/research-initiatives/research-centers-labs/center-advanced-study-teaching-and-learning/castl-measures) · [Primary psychometric paper](https://education.virginia.edu/media/17931)

### PRFQ: a research measure of the parent's reflective stance

- Eighteen items, three subscales; initially designed for parents of children 0–5. Initial internal consistencies were .70, .82, and .75, respectively. It is not a single relationship-quality score. Intervention responsiveness evidence was preliminary.
- UCL currently states that it is **available for research only and not yet suited for clinical use**. It lists a separate adolescent version for parents of children 12–18. Do not infer that the original, adolescent, infant, and translated versions are interchangeable or that all ages are equally covered.
- A commercial coach must obtain appropriate permission and independently justify its intended interpretation. An open-access validation article does not alone grant every deployment right.

[Primary validation](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0176218) · [Current instrument-owner guidance and versions](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/brain-sciences/pals/psychoanalysis/research/parental-reflective-functioning-questionnaire-prfq)

### P-PASS / POPS: useful for the child's perceived autonomy support

- P-PASS validation used two French-speaking, largely college-attending young-adult samples (N=210 and 315; mean ages 18.8 and 18.5). Its autonomy-supportive and controlling dimensions had high internal consistency, but cross-sectional shared-informant evidence is not proof of responsiveness, causation, or fit for young children.
- The author's lab offers distinct adolescent/young-adult, child, and parent-self-report versions. Select a specific version and verify its age, language, scoring, and evidence. Do not simply rewrite the college scale for a seven-year-old.
- POPS is completed by children about parents, with child and college forms. It is not a parent's self-evaluation.
- The Center for Self-Determination Theory requires **written permission for commercial use** of its copyrighted questionnaires. Research availability is not a commercial license.

[Primary P-PASS validation](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/2015_Mageau_etal_CanJofBehSci.pdf) · [Author's versions](https://www.mapageweb.umontreal.ca/mageaug/echelles.htm) · [POPS owner page](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/perceptions-of-parents-scales/) · [Commercial-use terms](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/questionnaires/)

### IPPA: adolescent perceived trust, communication, alienation

- Originally developed with 179 college students aged 16–20; subsequent adolescent/child adaptations require version-specific evidence. It can represent one young person's perceptions of specific relationships, not an objective relationship verdict or a toddler attachment classification.
- The author's website states that the IPPA remains copyrighted but permission is no longer required for use. The page was available in search results but direct fetch timed out; confirm the current terms and any adaptation/redistribution requirements before implementation.
- This review did not establish a daily-change interpretation or universally applicable clinical cutoff.

[Original primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02202939) · [Author's permissions statement](https://www.armsden.com/index.php) · [Author's versions](https://www.armsden.com/download.php)

### PACHIQ-R: another relationship candidate, with a content-fit warning

The original paper describes 21-item parent and 25-item child forms, with acceptance and conflict-resolution factors and Dutch clinical/community norms. Before selection, obtain the manual, exact age coverage, permissions, and longitudinal evidence. Some conflict-related content concerns obedience, so audit whether the selected outcome would inadvertently reward the very child-compliance model this product is rejecting. It is not an automatically suitable substitute for an alliance measure. [Primary validation](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1545-5300.2002.00709.x) · [Primary study discussing child-version adaptation](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00512/full)

### Observer measures: useful independent validation, higher burden

PICCOLO focuses on observable parenting in affection, responsiveness, encouragement, and teaching, with an owner-specified age range of 10–47 months. It is a practitioner observation tool with materials and training, not evidence that a language model can score home videos. It could inform a consented research study with trained, blinded raters; it should not become covert recording or automated child-state inference. Commercial digital rights require separate confirmation. [Publisher](https://brookespublishing.com/product/piccolo/) · [Primary validation in a Spanish sample](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00680/full)

### Instruments that must not be misnamed

- **PAM / Parenting Alliance Measure:** twenty items about cooperation, communication, and respect between parenting partners, for parents of children 1–19. Publisher reports alpha .97 and test–retest .80, but these do not validate a parent–child construct. Proprietary, qualification level B. [Publisher](https://www.parinc.com/products/PAM)
- **WAI / Working Alliance Inventory:** treatment/helping alliance, with permissions controlled by the Society for Psychotherapy Research. Replacing “therapist” with “parent” produces an adaptation needing new evidence and permission. [Owner's copyright statement](https://wai.profhorvath.com/copyright)
- **TASCP / parent–team alliance instruments:** parent/caregiver alliance with a therapist or clinical team, not the child. [Primary TASCP validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3647370/) · [Primary parent–team WAI adaptation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4605973/)

## 4. Is any composite defensible?

**Now: no substantiated universal “alliance out of 100.”** Averaging closeness, low conflict, reflective functioning, autonomy support, stress, and task completion would hide unlike constructs and informants. A high result could conceal a serious problem in one dimension. A low result could reflect honest reflection, a temporarily hard context, healthy disagreement, different reporting opportunities, or improved ability to notice mistakes.

A future narrowly defined score might be defensible for a specified research purpose after validation. First determine whether it represents one latent construct or an explicitly value-weighted index. Internal consistency alone cannot validate a formative index; correlation with satisfaction cannot validate the whole construct. Weights, missing-data behavior, uncertainty, subgroup performance, stability, responsiveness, and decision consequences must be justified. A 0–100 transformation creates no extra precision.

Do not import cutoffs or change thresholds from another context. Reliability of a single score, validity of a change score, and whether a change matters are different questions. COSMIN provides useful methodology, although a family-reflection product is not automatically a clinical patient-reported outcome instrument. [COSMIN study-design guidance](https://www.cosmin.nl/finding-right-tool/conducting-study-measurement-properties/) · [Primary COSMIN methods paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2848183/)

For research, separate informants. Do not average away disagreement or label one reporter wrong. A longitudinal primary study of 604 Mexican-immigrant-family adolescents found that direction and pattern of parent–adolescent discrepancy mattered; it cannot be reduced to “agreement is always healthy.” [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28689348/)

Changing item text, recall window (“generally” to “today”), respondent, response options, language, administration mode, scoring, age range, or combining selected items can alter validity. An author permitting a change does not establish measurement equivalence. A new AI-generated summary of questionnaire responses also needs validation; it is not merely the original test in a nicer interface.

### A constructive route to an original parent–child alliance instrument

A new instrument is possible to investigate; the present problem is insufficient evidence for the proposed interpretation, not a prohibition on measurement.

1. **Define the construct before the item pool.** Specify whether “alliance” means collaboration around one negotiated family activity, the child's felt experience of being understood, or a broader relationship process. Specify whose perspective it represents and why existing measures are inadequate. Do not assume that bond, goals, and tasks retain their therapy meaning in parenthood.
2. **Begin with qualitative parent and child work.** Include separate, safely conducted interviews, developmentally appropriate methods, dissenting views, disability/neurodivergence, diverse caregivers and cultures, and situations where parent and child legitimately want different things. Use expert developmental, clinical, safeguarding, and psychometric input without letting experts replace lived experience.
3. **Develop original content and check rights.** Map each item to a definition. Conduct cognitive interviews for comprehension, pressure, interpretive ambiguity, and unwanted incentives. If adapting existing content, get the relevant permission and document what changed. Parental readability is not sufficient evidence of child readability.
4. **Establish the measurement model.** Use exploratory work and independent confirmatory samples; test whether dimensions are distinct, whether a general factor is meaningful, and whether a total score loses important information. Assess ordinal response behavior, floor/ceiling effects, missingness, test–retest stability where stability is expected, and measurement error. High internal consistency is only one property.
5. **Validate the interpretation.** Test prespecified convergence and discrimination against appropriate external measures, independent observers, and child/parent reports; distinguish collaboration from compliance, satisfaction, stress, and social desirability. Test measurement invariance across relevant groups and across time. Validate externally, beyond the development team and development sample.
6. **Validate change and consequences.** Establish responsiveness and meaningful within-person change for the actual interval and population. Test whether showing the score helps decisions and outcomes more than plain-language reflection. Study unintended pressure, gaming, concealment, guilt, coercion, and misuse. Obtain independent ethics review for research involving families as appropriate.
7. **Limit claims to evidence.** A promising pilot is not population norms, diagnostic accuracy, app efficacy, or a universally comparable number. Until evidence supports individual interpretation, keep the instrument research-only. Never permit caregiver rankings or use as custody evidence.

This is a proposed development pathway informed by measurement principles, not a completed validation. Existing-scale licensing and validation of a new scale are separate workstreams.

## 5. Safer no-score UX proposal

These are proposed product ideas, not validated questionnaire items or treatment prescriptions.

1. **Choose one ordinary moment.** “Which moment would you like to handle differently?” Use optional context, not an exhaustive child profile. The parent chooses what to disclose.
2. **Separate what happened from what it might mean.** “What did you see or hear?” “What was happening for you?” “What else could explain it?” Include “I don't know.” Do not infer attachment, diagnosis, deception, or fixed personality.
3. **Identify the adult's adjustable part.** Pick one: pace/timing, wording, expectations, predictability, surroundings, available support, listening, or repair. Acknowledge constraints such as sleep, work, disability accommodations, safety, and lack of support without assigning blame.
4. **Keep a small multidimensional reflection.** Display ordinary-language notes under “Feeling heard,” “Room for choice,” “Clarity and support,” “Returning after a hard moment,” and “What I need to show up.” Do not turn these into radar charts, traffic lights, ranks, percentiles, or hidden grades.
5. **Try one bounded adjustment.** State the parent's action, where it applies, how to notice fit, and when to stop. The child's behavior is information, not a pass/fail score.
6. **Review with uncertainty.** “Helpful / mixed / not helpful / not enough opportunities / I'd change it.” Show concrete observations and counterexamples; never penalize missed logs. Ask whether the parent felt more able to act in line with their values, while separately checking burden or tension.
7. **Optional child voice, with a separate design gate.** If direct child participation is ever introduced, use age-appropriate assent, a genuine opt-out, clear privacy limits, and a safe review process. A parent-only account cannot claim to measure the child's experience. Do not silently invite children, record them, or expose private responses to caregivers.

No score is not no evaluation: the product can be rigorously evaluated in research without grading families in daily use.

Transparent tracking can still be useful. A parent may optionally record a factual count such as “I used my planned pause twice in three opportunities,” or privately rate “How supported did I feel today?” Such entries must be labeled as personal self-report, with the exact question and interval visible, and explicitly unvalidated if used as app-generated reflection items. Do not infer relationship quality from them, compare parents, hide a composite behind them, or imply that more logging means better parenting. Counts need a denominator and context; “no opportunities” differs from “did not try.” A user can also choose plain notes with no numbers at all.

### Example parent-adjustment experiments

| Situation | Parent-controlled adjustment | What could support or disconfirm the hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated friction leaving an activity | Give one predictable advance cue; offer two acceptable ways to finish | Note whether the parent gave the cue and whether the child reported/appeared less surprised. Mixed or unchanged response challenges the transition-warning explanation. Keep required safety limits. |
| Child stops talking when advice starts | Ask whether listening, help, or space would be useful; wait before offering solutions | Did the parent follow the preference? Was the invitation acceptable? A child choosing space is an informative outcome, not failure. |
| Parent raises their voice | After settling, briefly own the adult behavior and offer a restart without demanding a response | Did the adult take responsibility without blaming? Did the child want a different repair or more time? Immediate forgiveness is not the target. |
| Recurring disagreement over a routine | Explain the non-negotiable reason; invite the child to help choose implementation within real limits | Can the parent maintain the boundary while hearing disagreement? If options are not genuinely available, revise the invitation rather than manufacture choice. |
| Adult feels overloaded before pickup | Try a feasible brief pause or arrange support before the interaction | Did the change reduce the adult's rushing or make listening more feasible? If it adds burden, simplify or stop. |

These are low-stakes reflection examples, not N-of-1 causal proof. Do not randomize withdrawal of comfort, safety, necessary accommodation, or care. Stop if an experiment increases distress, pressure, conflict, or surveillance.

## 6. Misuse and bias safeguards

- **No parent blame:** parent agency is a lever, not a claim that parents cause all difficulties or can solve structural constraints. Offer achievable changes and support needs, not perfection.
- **No child optimization:** do not reward quietness, obedience, productivity, affection, confession, or fast emotional recovery as “alliance.”
- **No app pleasing:** avoid daily relationship grades, streaks, badges for “good parenting,” push notifications warning a bond is declining, or optimization toward engagement.
- **No mind-reading:** show the evidentiary source of interpretations and allow correction; avoid presenting guesses as findings.
- **No caregiver/sibling comparison:** differences in time, responsibilities, context, and reports are not a ranking of love or skill. Avoid custody, employment, school-placement, or access-to-care uses.
- **No false reassurance:** a favorable reflection cannot certify safety. Concerning disclosures need an appropriate professional/safety pathway, not a better score or a generic experiment.
- **No universal cultural ideal:** recruit across languages, family structures, disability/neurodivergence, caregiver gender, socioeconomic context, and cultural expectations; test differential item functioning rather than assuming translated wording is equivalent.
- **Privacy by minimization:** prefer adult-owned descriptions and optional local notes; no passive audio/video, covert child diary ingestion, or medical/temperament profiles merely to enable generic suggestions. Any later child-data research needs a separately designed ethics, privacy, and jurisdiction-specific legal review.

## 7. Falsifiable validation plan

### Before outcome claims

1. Choose one developmental band, setting, user group, language, and intended use. Involve developmental researchers, measurement specialists, caregivers, and appropriately supported youth advisers. Define what the product explicitly does not infer.
2. Conduct cognitive interviews and adversarial usability sessions. Can people distinguish a tentative interpretation from a fact? Do prompts invite child control, guilt, surveillance, or conflict? Revise before expanding.
3. Select licensed, age-appropriate external measures and an independent observation protocol. Pre-register hypotheses, primary outcomes, missing-data handling, adverse outcomes, and analysis. Determine sample size from realistic effects, clustering, attrition, and the precision needed; do not invent an arbitrary “validation N.”

### Test the mechanism, not just liking the app

- **Primary hypothesis:** compared with a matched active-control reflection tool, the app improves a prespecified parent-behavior outcome measured by blinded observation or an independent, appropriate informant.
- **Necessary child-experience check:** gains in parent self-report should not coexist with worse child-reported pressure, lower sense of being heard, or more relationship tension. A parent-only product can make a parent-report claim, not silently upgrade it to the child's experience.
- **Personalization hypothesis:** context-sensitive suggestions outperform the same evidence-informed suggestions without temperament labels, at equal time and attention. If added profiling gives no incremental benefit, remove it and its data collection.
- **No-score hypothesis:** reflective UX performs at least as well on behavior and better on pressure/guilt than a score-centered experimental prototype. Score testing belongs under appropriate research review; no need to expose unvalidated scores in production.
- **Uncertainty hypothesis:** users retain awareness that child-state explanations are tentative and can generate plausible alternatives rather than becoming more overconfident.
- **Equity and access:** estimate outcomes and error patterns across prespecified groups; underpowered subgroup results should remain uncertain rather than declared equivalent.

Use parent-level/family-level allocation as appropriate, blinded outcome assessment where feasible, intention-to-treat analysis, preregistered follow-up, and separate feasibility from efficacy. App usage, retention, positive reviews, knowledge acquisition, questionnaire alpha, and falling conflict counts are insufficient efficacy evidence by themselves.

### Explicit decisions that could falsify the concept

- If parent satisfaction improves but independent behavior and child experience do not, drop the relationship-improvement claim.
- If personalization performs no better than simpler reflection, simplify and reduce data collection.
- If increased child compliance explains apparent gains while pressure or child voice worsens, the mechanism conflicts with the product's purpose.
- If the score adds no benefit, worsens guilt, or changes reporting rather than behavior, do not ship it.
- If effects depend on trained human support, describe and preserve that dependence; do not attribute them to an autonomous app.
- If measurements lack cross-time equivalence or change precision, do not display individual progress claims.

## Research prompt addendum

Investigate an iteration-2 parent-support product with this non-negotiable premise: understanding a child is useful insofar as it helps the PARENT adapt how they show up, while respecting the child's agency, development, privacy, and legitimate need for boundaries and support. The product must not label or optimize the child, covertly infer their mind, or maximize compliance.

Evaluate the hypothesis that a therapeutic-alliance analogy can organize reflection about being understood, shared understanding, voice, and repair. Distinguish this analogy from a validated measurement claim. Separate therapy working alliance, parent–child relationship quality, attachment security, attachment disorders, parental reflective functioning, autonomy support, goodness of fit, satisfaction, and co-parent alliance. Explicitly explain why Parenting Alliance Measure/Inventory and parent–therapist alliance instruments do not directly measure parent–child alliance.

Use primary studies, original measure manuals or owner pages, and authoritative assessment guidance. For each relevant measure, report the exact version, construct, respondent, age range, language/sample, reliability, validity, measurement invariance, observational convergence, test–retest/measurement error, responsiveness, individual-change interpretation, and available commercial/adaptation permissions. Mark unknowns rather than filling gaps. Prioritize CPRS, PRFQ/PRFQ-I/PRFQ-A, P-PASS/POPS, IPPA, PACHIQ-R, and appropriate observer tools. Do not reproduce proprietary item sets.

Assess whether a single composite is psychometrically and ethically defensible for this precise purpose. Identify whether a proposed measure is reflective or formative and what would justify weighting, aggregation, missing-data rules, confidence intervals, and individual interpretation. Explain why a 0–100 presentation, high alpha, or correlation with satisfaction does not establish validity. Do not create example family scores or imply that an adapted scale retains validation automatically.

Propose a no-score, multidimensional alternative that separates observed events, parent interpretations, the child's own words when voluntarily supplied, contextual constraints, and one feasible parent-controlled adjustment. Include small experiments involving timing, listening, bounded choice, clear explanations, environmental accommodations, and repair. Make success compatible with a child disagreeing, needing time, declining to talk, or keeping a boundary. Avoid adult demands for reassurance or forgiveness.

Design preregistered, falsifiable research comparing the product with a matched active control and testing whether personalization adds benefit over a simpler, lower-data alternative. Include independently assessed parent behavior, age-appropriate child experience where ethically feasible, reporting bias, missingness, burden, guilt, conflict, and adverse effects. Specify findings that would require dropping the score, removing profiling, limiting claims, adding trained human support, or stopping the intervention. Distinguish observational association, intervention evidence, measurement evidence, product inference, and untested product hypotheses throughout.
