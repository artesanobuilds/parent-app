# Naming Recommendation: "Onda", a Parenting Companion for Getting on Your Child's Wavelength

**Recommendation: name the product Onda.** It's short and warm, it works in English and Spanish, and it carries the product's promise inside a common Spanish idiom: *estar en la misma onda*, "to be on the same wavelength." Onda isn't cleared yet. The U.S. software trademark register already has several ONDA filings, so a lawyer's clearance search has to come before any public commitment. **Understory** is the strongest backup if the U.S. English market comes first.

## TL;DR
- **Onda wins on fit, warmth and bilingual reach.** "Onda" means "wave" in Spanish, Portuguese and Italian. *Buena onda* ("good vibes, a good person") and *¿qué onda?* ("what's up?", informal in Mexico) make it friendly and a little playful. *En la misma onda* says "I understand my child," and the wave image covers ups, downs and rhythm. It doesn't say parent, mom, AI, fix or score, so it works for grandparents, guardians, foster and adoptive parents, and families with neurodivergent kids.
- **The category is crowded, and that rules out most of the obvious names.** Attune is already the name of at least two parenting apps. Lantern is a nonprofit parenting text service in English, Spanish and Arabic. Kindsight, Kinsights, Kinwise, Wavelength, Glimmer, Wonderly, Wonderkin, Duet and Lazo are all taken in the same or nearby categories. Lazo is worse than taken: it's tied to family location tracking and a "loyalty test" app for checking whether partners cheat.
- **Before you commit:** (1) commission a full U.S. clearance search plus EUIPO, Mexico (IMPI) and Spain (OEPM) searches on Onda and Understory; (2) run the bilingual parent test described below (no parent research was done for this report); (3) keep Understory and Ajá ready as fallbacks.

## Key Findings

### 1. What the competition is called, and the opening

**Verified (store listings and press, 2025–2026):**
- **Descriptive "Parent/Child + AI/Coach" names dominate:**
  - ChildMind AI: Parenting Coach (Google Play, March 2026)
  - Nuna: AI Parenting Coach
  - ParentPal: AI Parenting Coach
  - Wizzer: Parenting Coach, which grows a "tree" as parents complete milestones (updated July 2026)
  - Parenta AI, aimed at neurodivergent families
  - Coddle, an "AI parenting companion" for babies
  - ARIA ("Advanced Responsive Intelligent Assistant for Parenting")
  - Peaceful Discipline's "AI Parenting App," announced for 2026
  - Mamma & Daddy (UK, waitlist)
  - TinyPal
  - Oath Care's ParentGPT, which Fast Company reports has shut down
- **Human persona names front the AI:**
  - Good Inside's chatbot is "GiGi." Fortune (Claire Zillman, Feb 27, 2026) reports Good Inside "is profitable and generated $34 million in revenue last year, a nearly 50% increase year over year," with more than 60 employees and more than 100,000 subscribers paying about $25 a month.
  - Joy's assistant is "Emily." Forbes (Sindhya Valloppillil, Nov 12, 2025) reported a "$14 million Series A, co-led by Raga Partners and Forerunner Ventures," with "50,000 paying members already on its Joy Parenting Club app."
  - CoupleWork's AI coach is "Maxine."
- **Attunement and light metaphors are taken:**
  - "Attune – The Parenting App" (DLG Software) records parenting moments and sends coaching for ages 2–6 at $19.99/month.
  - A separate Attune at attune-ai-app.com targets parents of neurodiverse children.
  - "Attunement: Parenting Tools & Hacks" is in beta.
  - Lantern, formerly Bright by Text, sends free parenting texts in English, Spanish or Arabic; its ColoradoGives.org profile says it is "currently serving more than 450,000 families nationwide." There's also a separate "Lantern – help for parents" app for autistic children.
  - Glimmer is used by a family task app built for neurodivergent households, by an AI journaling app and by a kids' skills app.
- **"Kin-" names are saturated:**
  - Kindsight is a large nonprofit fundraising software brand.
  - Kinsights has been a parenting advice network since 2011.
  - "Kinwise" is used by a relationship tracker with a "Connection Score," an in-laws memory app, a $99/year family story archive, a teacher-coaching AI and hackathon projects.
- **Relationship-tool names lean on pairs and warmth:** Paired, Lasting, Flamme (formerly Sparks), Maia, Relish, Cuddle, Lainie and Between. Co-parenting tools lean on fairness and records: BestInterest, OurFamilyWizard, AppClose, TalkingParents.
- **Crowded promise language** (interpretation from the listings): "calm," "fewer meltdowns," "judgment-free," "24/7," "in your pocket," "rebuilding the village," "expert-backed."

**Interpretation: where the opening is.** Almost every competitor names either the user (*parent*), the technology (*AI*, *GPT*) or the problem (*tantrums*, *calm*). Almost none names the *relationship* between parent and child. That's the gap. A name about being in tune with each other, not about managing behavior, stands apart and matches the "understand → adjust → reconnect" loop. No competitor I found has a bilingual name built on a native Spanish idiom, even though Lantern's Spanish-language service shows the audience is there.

### 2. Why Onda beats the alternatives

- **The meaning is the promise.** *En la misma onda* is exactly "help me understand my child." Parents don't need it explained.
- **It's playful without being childish.** *Buena onda* and *¿qué onda?* are casual and friendly. They feel like a person, not a brand.
- **English speakers can say and spell it.** It's two syllables, ON-dah, with no hidden meaning. English speakers who've heard "wave" in Spanish, Portuguese or Italian get the image; those who haven't hear something soft and easy.
- **It fits the whole product.** Waves cover hard moments and joyful ones, repair after a rough patch, and practice through repetition. There's no sense of control, scoring or diagnosis.
- **It doesn't box anyone in.** It doesn't name a gender, age, family structure or technology, and it could later stretch to teachers, co-parents or teens.

**Its risks are real:**
- **Trademark crowding** is the biggest one (see screening below).
- **"Onda" and "Honda" sound identical in Spanish**, because the h is silent. Spoken word-of-mouth needs a test.
- **In Spain**, Onda Cero is the No. 3 national radio network, with 2,265,000 daily listeners in the 2nd wave of Spain's 2026 EGM audience survey (Barlovento Comunicación report), behind SER and COPE. Onda Regional de Murcia is public radio, so "Onda + descriptor" is a common broadcast pattern there.
- **"¿Qué onda?" is strongly Mexican and Central American.** Spanish speakers from Spain or the Southern Cone will read it as Latin American; that's neutral to positive, but still worth testing.

## Ranked Top Five

### #1: Onda (44/50)
- **Pronunciation:** EN "ON-dah" (/ˈɒn.də/ or /ˈɑːn.də/). ES "ÓN-da" (/ˈon.da/).
- **Meaning:** "wave." *En la misma onda* = on the same wavelength; *buena onda* = good vibes.
- **Strengths:**
  - The promise is built into a real idiom.
  - Warm, short and gender-neutral.
  - Spanish speakers understand it instantly; English speakers can say it.
  - Doesn't say parent, AI, fix, perfect or control.
- **Risks:**
  - Crowded register.
  - Sounds like "Honda" when spoken in Spanish.
  - Spanish radio namesakes.
  - No meaning for English speakers unless the tagline supplies one.
- **Screening (preliminary, search-snippet level, October 2026):**
  - *USPTO:* ONDA, serial 79383381, filed Aug 3, 2023 by Onda Holding S.A., covers computer software and mobile applications (Class 9 implied; current status not checked). It's the most important possible blocker. ONDA, serial 98208228 (Class 42, energy-management software, filed Oct 2023, status unknown). ONDAS, Reg. 6635131 (Class 9 medical software, registered Feb 2022). Older ONDA marks (73740701, 73740700, 74389828) are dead.
  - *App stores:* "ONDA" is a members-club app, "Onda" is a Mexican auction app, and "ONDA Surf" is an AI surf forecaster using getonda.ai. ONDA TV (Honduras), Onda Regional (Murcia) and "Onda" payments in Paraguay/Argentina also exist. None of these is a parenting, family or AI-companion app.
  - *Domains:* I couldn't verify who owns onda.com or onda.app. Avoid "getonda," because ONDA Surf already uses getonda.ai. Check variants such as ondafamily.app, onda.family, meetonda.com and hellonda.com. None were checked.
  - *International:* EUIPO, OEPM (Spain) and IMPI (Mexico) not checked.
- **Tagline:** "Get on their wavelength." (ES: "En la misma onda.")

### #2: Understory (38/50)

  - *USPTO:* UNDERSTORY serials 97594234 (Class 9) and 97594239, both filed Sep 2022 by Understory Technologies Corp. Status not checked.
  - *Apps:* "Understory: Reading Tracker" and a 2026 Mac notes app called Understory. No parenting app.
  - *Domains:* understory.com/.app owners not verified.
- **Tagline:** "The story under the moment."

### #3: Ajá (38/50)
- **Pronunciation:** ES "ah-HA" (/aˈxa/). In English, without the accent, it risks being read as "AH-juh" or "AY-juh."
- **Meaning:** Spanish for "aha / I see / uh-huh." It's the sound of understanding clicking.
- **Strengths:**
  - The most playful name in the top five.
  - Captures the "reconsider your interpretation" moment.
  - Familiar to anyone who knows "aha."
  - Fewest conflicts found in my screening.
- **Risks:**
  - The accent mark will get dropped in URLs and app search.
  - Without the accent, "Aja" collides with AJA Video Systems, which holds about 18 Class 9 marks, and with "Aja – Nutrition Tracker," an AI meal-scanning wellness app.
  - English pronunciation is unpredictable.
  - Can sound flippant when a parent is distressed.
- **Screening:** AJÁ/AHA in Classes 41/44/45 not searched. aja.app not verified. aha.io (Aha! product software) not assessed.
- **Tagline:** "For the moments it clicks."

### #4: Mirada (38/50, not screened)
- **Pronunciation:** EN "mih-RAH-dah." ES "mi-RÁ-da."
- **Meaning:** Spanish for "a look, a gaze, a way of seeing." *Una nueva mirada* = a fresh perspective.
- **Strengths:**
  - Easy and elegant in both languages.
  - Directly about seeing your child differently.
  - Adult and credible.
- **Risks:**

### #5: Hilo (35/50, highest conflict risk)
- **Pronunciation:** EN "HEE-loh" (often read "HY-loh," like the Hawaiian city). ES "Í-lo" (silent h).
- **Meaning:** Spanish for "thread." *Retomar el hilo* = to pick up the thread again, a good fit for repair and continuity.

## Names Rejected and Why

- **MVP / Most Valued Parent:** "MVP" suggests ranking and performance, which is the parenting-scorecard framing the product rejects. It's a sports acronym that most Spanish speakers won't decode, and "Most Valued" sets parents up to compare themselves with others.
- **Attune / Attunement:** a perfect fit, but at least three parenting products already use it, including a paid coaching app for ages 2–6 that records family moments.
- **Lantern:** an established nonprofit parenting text service that says it is "currently serving more than 450,000 families nationwide" in English, Spanish or Arabic (ColoradoGives.org profile), plus a separate parenting app for autistic children.
- **Wavelength:** a popular party-game app by Palm Court LLC, rated "4.9 out of 5, 64K Ratings" with an Editors' Choice award on its U.S. App Store listing, plus a dating app. Onda carries the same idea without the conflict.
- **Kindsight / Kinsight / Kinwise:** Kindsight is a major fundraising-software brand. Kinsights is an existing parenting network. Kinwise is used by several relationship and family products, one of which scores connections.
- **Lazo** (ES "bond/tie"): family location-tracking and safety apps (surveillance), a viral "loyalty test" app for catching cheaters, and a Spanish couples app.
- **Glimmer, Wonderly, Wonderkin, Duet:** each is used by several apps. Duet is also tied to divorce and co-parenting services.
- **Anything with "AI," "GPT," "Coach," "Pal" or "Parent-":** these are the most crowded patterns, they put the technology ahead of the relationship, and "Parent" leaves out grandparents and guardians who don't call themselves parents.
- **Tether, Radar, Monitor, Compass-style control names:** they suggest surveillance or steering the child.
- **Mend, Fix, Repair-first names:** they imply the child or relationship is broken.
- **Super-, Perfect-, Pro-, Ace- names:** they imply perfect parenting and invite comparison.
- **Village:** already a category cliché. Joy's president, Emily Greenberg, said in its Nov 12, 2025 Business Wire release that "every parent now has a village of experts—supercharged with AI—available in their pocket 24/7," and Forbes headlined Joy as "Rebuilding The Village For Modern Families."
- **Gentle / Peaceful / Positive-:** tied to specific parenting ideologies, and some parents hear them as preachy.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt Onda as the working name now,** and give it an English meaning through the tagline ("Get on their wavelength").
2. **Clear it legally before launch.**
   - Start with Onda Holding S.A.'s Class 9 filing (serial 79383381): live status, goods, and whether the owner is active in the U.S.
   - Then run full knockout and clearance searches in Classes 9, 41, 42, 44 and 45 at the USPTO, EUIPO/WIPO Madrid, IMPI (Mexico) and OEPM (Spain).
   - A distinctive logo and a composite mark (for example, "Onda Family" or "Onda: on the same wavelength") may help if the word mark alone is contested. Ask counsel.
3. **Secure digital assets:** check and register sensible variants (onda.family, ondafamily.app, meetonda.com) and matching social handles. Don't use "getonda," because ONDA Surf uses getonda.ai.
4. **Hold Understory as the English-first fallback and Ajá as the playful bilingual fallback.** Screen Mirada before testing it.
5. **Give the AI a role, not a persona.** Competitors lean on named personas like GiGi, Emily and Maxine. The product is about the parent–child relationship, so the companion can just be "Onda" rather than a character the parent bonds with.

## Parent-Testing Plan

**No parent research was done for this report. Nothing here is real parent feedback.**

**Who to recruit (minimum):**
- **Qualitative:** 24–30 sixty-minute interviews, roughly half in English and half in Spanish, with Spanish speakers from both Mexico/U.S. and Spain.
- **Quantitative:** a 400–600 person monadic online survey (each person sees one name), split between U.S. English, U.S. Hispanic/Mexico and Spain.
- **Set quotas for:** fathers, single parents, grandparents raising grandchildren, foster/adoptive parents and kinship guardians, parents of neurodivergent children, lower-income households and parents without a college degree, and ages 22–65+.
- **Recognize the limits:** an online panel over-represents digitally comfortable parents, so add community-organization recruitment to reach the others.

**Test materials:** Onda, Understory, Ajá, Mirada and Hilo, each shown alone first, then with its tagline, then in an app-icon mock-up. Rotate the order and include one control (a descriptive name such as "Parent Coach").

**Questions to ask, in order:**
1. *Spontaneous meaning:* "What do you think a product called ___ does? Who is it for?"
2. *Pronunciation:* "Please say the name out loud." (Record it.) Then play a recording and ask: "How would you spell what you just heard?"
3. *Emotional response:* "Pick three words for how this name makes you feel." Also ask for agreement (1–7) with: warm, trustworthy, fun, childish, clinical, judgmental, preachy, "feels like it's watching my child."
4. *Fit to the promise:* show "Helps you understand your child so you can change how you show up," then ask: "How well does ___ fit this?" (1–7).
5. *Inclusion:* "Does this feel made for someone like you and your family? Why or why not?"
6. *Friend test:* "Finish this sentence aloud: 'This app called ___ helped me understand my kid better.' How comfortable would you be saying that to a friend?" (1–7)
7. *Trust in hard moments:* "Imagine a really hard night with your child. Would you open an app called ___? What would make you hesitate?"
8. *Associations:* "Does this name remind you of any other company, product or word?" Probe for Honda, Onda Cero, Hilo, AJA and Aha.
9. *Recall:* the next day, ask unprompted: "Which parenting app names do you remember?" Then ask them to spell it.
10. *Preference:* after all exposures, ask which name they'd pick and why, and which one they'd reject outright.

**Decision rule:** keep Onda unless (a) unaided Spanish-language recall or spelling is clearly worse than the alternatives (the Honda confusion), (b) English speakers find it meaningless even with the tagline, or (c) any priority caregiver group rates it markedly lower on "made for someone like me."

## Caveats and Unresolved Uncertainties

- **Trademark screening was preliminary.** It relied on search snippets from Justia and Trademarkia, not live TSDR records. Status, ownership and the scope of goods for every serial number cited need checking. This is not legal clearance. EUIPO, IMPI and OEPM were not searched, and Classes 44 and 45 were not checked for any name.
- **Domain ownership was mostly not verified.** A registered domain is not the same as an active competing business, and this report doesn't treat it as one. The exception is hilo.com, which is clearly in active use by a wellness product.
- **Onda Cero's audience** is confirmed by Spain's 2026 EGM survey (No. 3 network, 2,265,000 daily listeners in the 2nd wave, per the Barlovento Comunicación report), but its trademark scope in Spain was not searched and should be confirmed.
- **Mirada and most of the 30 candidates were not screened.**
- **What would change the recommendation:**
  - A live, active Class 9 or 42 ONDA registration covering consumer apps in the U.S. or Mexico.
  - Parent testing showing poor English comprehension or Spanish "Honda" confusion.
  - Evidence that "¿qué onda?" reads as too slangy or flippant to grandparents or Spain-based parents.
  - If the launch market is English-only U.S., Understory's precision may matter more than Onda's bilingual warmth.
