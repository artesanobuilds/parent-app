# Three critics on the first draft

Each pass read the draft cold and argued against it. What follows is what they said and what
actually changed. Criticism that didn't survive is recorded too, with the reason.

---

## Tony Fadell

*"Where's the product? Sixteen slides and I haven't seen the thing."*

1. **No screenshot anywhere.** Sixteen slides of argument about a product that exists and is on
   TestFlight, and a partner never sees it. For a consumer product this is disqualifying. You have
   fifteen real screenshots sitting in the repo.
2. **Slide 05 is an org chart.** "Five doors" is how *you* think about the system. No parent wakes
   up needing the Camps door. They wake up at 11pm having remembered something. Lead with the
   moment, then reveal that the same agent covers all five.
3. **Slide 07 is a spec sheet.** "567 tests" doesn't move a consumer investor, and the number that
   should be on that slide isn't. The detail that proves obsessive care is this: when the agent
   pulls a deadline out of a school PDF, it shows you *the exact sentence it came from*. That one
   behavior tells a parent the machine isn't making things up. It's worth more than the test count.
4. **The first five minutes are missing.** What happens the first time she opens it? If the answer
   is "she fills out a profile," you have a product problem, not a deck problem.

**Changed:** Added slide 06, a full-bleed product slide with real screenshots. Rewrote 05 to open on
the 11pm moment. Promoted the show-me-the-sentence detail to the front of the credibility slide.

**Not changed:** The first-five-minutes slide. It's a real gap but it belongs in the demo, not in a
pre-seed deck that's already long. Flagged for the follow-up conversation.

---

## Steve Jobs

*"You have three slides here. Why are there sixteen?"*

1. **Cut forty percent of the words.** Nearly every card is two sentences where one would hit
   harder. A card is a headline, not a paragraph.
2. **Slides 02 and 03 are the same slide** told twice — agentic AI arrived, families didn't get it;
   families are unmanaged, everyone else got software. Pick the better one. The better one is 02.
3. **"Four things a fast follower does not get in a quarter"** is a title written by someone who is
   scared. You don't describe your moat by what the other guy lacks. Say what you have.
4. **"AI chief of staff"** is language borrowed from a job no parent has ever held. It is a
   consultant's phrase. A parent doesn't want a chief of staff. They want to stop being the only
   person who remembers.
5. **Slide 12 has three columns and one idea.** The idea is: everyone else needs your ex to sign up.
   Delete the other two columns and let that sit on a white field.

**Changed:** Retitled slide 01 around remembering, not org-chart language. Retitled the moat slide
to "What compounds." Cut ~35% of body copy across all cards. Slide 12 reduced to the one line, with
the other two comparisons demoted to a single supporting row.

**Not changed:** Merging 02 and 03. They do different jobs — 02 is the thesis, 03 is the pain. What
was true is that 03 was arguing rather than showing, so it's now three statistics and nothing else.
Also kept sixteen slides: the appendix-free deck has to answer the moat and the model, and a
pre-seed partner who won't read sixteen won't write the check either.

---

## Ben Horowitz

*"This is a good business. Tell me why it's a big company."*

1. **$12.9M ARR is a nice outcome, not a venture one.** Your own GTM document says the base case is
   roughly $300K ARR at 24 months. If that number is in your head, it will be in your voice. Either
   you believe the larger case or you shouldn't be raising venture money for this.
2. **"Why doesn't OpenAI just ship this" is unanswered.** It's implied on the moat slide. Implied
   isn't answered. Put the answer on the page: they will not take on child-safety liability, they
   don't have the family graph, and distribution in this category runs on trust, not on model
   quality.
3. **The corpses cut both ways.** You use Milo and Yohana as timing evidence. A skeptic reads the
   same fact as "this category kills companies, including well-funded ones." Address it directly or
   it becomes the objection you get in the partner meeting after you leave the room.
4. **The CTO is employed full-time.** Miguel is VP Engineering at SonderMind. Every investor will
   ask, and the deck is silent. Silence on that reads as hiding it.
5. **Where does this go after camps?** Camps is a wedge. The company is only big if the wedge opens
   onto the whole household operations job. Say what the second and third act are.

**Changed:** Added slide 09, "Why this gets big," carrying the venture-scale path and the
OpenAI answer as its own card. Added the category-corpses objection directly to the why-now slide
rather than leaving it to be discovered. Expansion path stated explicitly.

**Not changed, escalated to Miguel instead:** the full-time-employment question. That is a founder
decision about commitment and timing, not a copywriting decision, and it does not belong on a slide
until he decides what the answer is. It is the single most likely question he gets asked, so he
should have the sentence ready before this deck goes out.

---

## What all three agreed on

Nobody attacked the wedge, the safety architecture, or the evidence base. All three attacked the
same thing from different angles: **the deck was arguing when it should have been showing.** Every
revision above is a version of that one note.
