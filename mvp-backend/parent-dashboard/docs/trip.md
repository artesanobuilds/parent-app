# Family trips

A family trip is the one plan that touches everything else: the kids are
covered those days, the other parent needs to see the dates, and there's a
small mountain of prep before you leave. MVP handles exactly that slice —
**dates, both calendars, packing — not bookings.**

## Get the dates on the plan

Say it the way you'd say it out loud:

> "We're taking the kids to Tahoe June 14 through 18."

That lands on the board as one card with its real range ("Jun 14 – Jun 18").
Those days count as covered — a trip week will never show up in your
coverage gaps as if the kids had nowhere to be.

## Both calendars

The assistant adds an all-day calendar entry for each kid across the trip's
dates. If you've connected Google Calendar, those entries sync out like
everything else — so a co-parent looking at the shared calendar sees the
trip without anyone sending a text.

## The prep, handled

When you mark the trip **booked**, three to-dos appear on their own:

- **Packing list** — start it early; the night-before scramble is optional.
- **Travel consent letter** — if one parent is traveling alone with the
  kids, a signed consent letter from the other parent avoids questions at
  airports and borders.
- **Meds & documents** — medications, insurance cards, and each kid's ID or
  passport.

Check them off as you go, like any other to-do.

## What MVP won't do

MVP doesn't plan itineraries, book flights or hotels, or pick destinations —
that's your trip to design. Ask for any of that and the assistant will say
so plainly and offer to get the dates on the plan instead.
