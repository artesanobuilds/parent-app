# The parenting coach

The coach handles the hard parts of parenting — the bedtime meltdowns, the
sibling wars, the "am I doing this right?" at 11pm — **and** the logistics
behind them: the camps, the classes, the calendar, the to-dos. It used to be
two separate assistants; it's one now. It lives in the iPhone app, in the
**Coach** tab.

That matters for the turns that are both at once. "Ben's summer is a mess" is a
coverage problem *and* an overwhelmed parent, and you get one answer to the
whole thing instead of being sent to another tab for the other half. Everything
on [the planning assistant](planner-chat) page is something you can ask the
coach directly, and the two share one memory — tell one, and the other knows.

## What a conversation looks like

Bring it a real moment, the way you'd tell a friend:

![The coach answers with something you can do tonight — not a lecture.](/dashboard/docs-assets/ios-coach.png)

The coach's style is deliberate: name what's actually happening, then give
you two or three concrete things to try *tonight*, sized to your kid's age.
No parenting-book chapters, no judgment.

## It knows your family — because you told it

The coach reads the same family profile as everything else in MVP: your
kids' ages, temperaments, what's been hard lately. When you mention something
new — "the meltdowns started this week" — it quietly updates the profile, so
next week's conversation starts from where you actually are, not from zero.

You can see and edit everything it knows in **Profile**. Nothing is inferred
from anywhere else; the profile is what you've said, and it's yours to
correct or delete.

It also knows your **Circle** — your co-parent, your nanny, grandma, whoever
you've added — including the notes you keep on each of them. So "can Rosa cover
Thursday?" is a question it can actually answer.

## Reading, when it's useful

There's no library to browse any more. The coach knows the whole library and
brings you an article when one genuinely fits what you're going through —
usually one link, in the middle of a sentence, not a reading list. The same
ranking powers **Home → For you → See all** if you'd rather go looking.

## Your past conversations

Conversations are kept. Tap the clock icon at the top of the **Coach** tab and
you'll see everything you've talked about, newest first, each with a short title
and a line about what came of it — *"Bedtime battles — talked through Lily's
bedtime pushback"*. Tap one to open it and carry on where you left off; the
coach still has the whole thread in mind.

The pencil icon starts a **new** conversation. It no longer throws the old one
away — that's what the history list is for. Use it when you're changing the
subject and don't want the last thing bleeding into the next one.

Starting fresh on the Coach tab? You'll get three suggestions to tap — one of
them uses your own child's name. Tapping one just sends it, so you don't have to
think of an opening line.

## Voice

The waveform button starts a voice conversation with the same coach — useful
when your hands are full, which is most of the time. Voice calls are their own
thing and don't show up in the typed history list.

## What it isn't

The coach is an AI, and the app says so right under the input: it can make
mistakes, and **it is not a crisis line**. For emergencies or concerns about
anyone's safety, contact local emergency services or a crisis hotline.
