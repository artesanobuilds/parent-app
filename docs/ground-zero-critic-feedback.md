# Ground Zero — Critic Feedback

Before finalizing the Ground Zero document, we ran it through three critical lenses to pressure-test the strategy and identify blind spots.

---

## Tony Fadell (Build discipline)

"You have a working product. That's rare at this stage—most teams are still arguing over wireframes. But I see scope creep hiding in plain sight. Calendar integration, tablet support, homeschool market, a senior app—you named six different products in one meeting. The iron rule: if it doesn't make the beta demo better, it doesn't exist yet. Write it on an index card and tape it to your monitor."

"The voice pipeline at 150ms is genuinely impressive. But 'moderation' that works 'as designed' is not the same as 'moderation that works.' You need ten families breaking this thing before you know if it works. Not fifty—ten. Recruit them today, not in fall."

"The one-two step Greg mentioned—that's the only thing that matters for the first 30 seconds. A parent opens the app. What happens? If the answer takes more than two sentences to explain, redesign it until it doesn't."

---

## Steve Jobs (Product soul)

"The Hearth design system is thoughtful. But I want to ask: what is the one screenshot that makes someone say 'I need this'? Not the feature list. Not the architecture. The single image. You don't have it yet—and you need it before you talk to a single investor."

"The kid companion is either a feature or it's the product. You need to decide. If a parent opens this app and the first thing they do is set up their child's companion, that's a different company than one where they browse articles. Both are valid. Doing both equally is not."

"'Most Valuable Parent' is not a name I'd ship. It sounds like a sports trophy. The name should make a tired parent at 11pm feel seen, not evaluated. Keep looking."

---

## Joe Hudson (Executive coaching lens)

"Three founders finding each other at this moment—that's not an accident. Miguel built the thing alone for months. Heather held the vision for years. Greg brings the 'what is this actually for?' question. The team's shadow risk isn't technical—it's the unspoken assumption that 'keeping Miguel focused' means Miguel shouldn't have a voice in product direction. Name that tension now, before it festers."

"The meeting notes show everyone excited and agreeing. That's dangerous at this stage. Your next meeting needs one real disagreement—voiced, explored, resolved. If you can't disagree about what the beta should NOT include, you'll ship everything and finish nothing."

"The parenting space attracts people who want to help. That's beautiful, and it's a trap. 'Helping parents' is infinite scope. 'Helping this specific parent with this specific problem tonight' is a product. Every time you catch yourself saying 'we could also...', ask: 'but will we?' And then: 'by when?'"

---

## Adjustments made to the Ground Zero document based on this feedback

- Strengthened the "must-ship for beta" vs "future" distinction throughout the document. If it doesn't improve the beta demo, it's explicitly labeled as post-beta.
- Added concrete success criteria for the beta (Section 8)—not just "launch by fall" but measurable outcomes.
- Elevated the "what is the hero screenshot?" question as an open design task for Greg.
- Acknowledged the kid-companion-vs-coaching-agent prioritization question explicitly rather than implying both are equal.
- Added a note about early recruiting (10 families now, not 50 in fall).
