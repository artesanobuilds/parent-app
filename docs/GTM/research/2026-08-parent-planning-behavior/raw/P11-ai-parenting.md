# P11 — AI + Parenting: adoption, trust, the free-alternative problem, willingness to pay

Collector P11. Compiled 2026-08-21. US-only unless flagged. Follows BRIEF.md tiers.

**Confidence note up front:** adoption and platform-capability evidence is strong (Tier A/B, 2026).
Trust evidence is thin and contradictory. Willingness-to-pay evidence *specific to family/household AI*
essentially does not exist — I found none at Tier A/B/C and say so rather than inventing it.

## Interpretation

**1. Adoption is not the problem; it is the threat.** Parents are the heaviest consumer-AI cohort in
America — 79% used AI in six months vs 54% of non-parents, 29% daily vs 15% (#10). Millennial parents
(30–49) have the highest *daily* rate of any age band, 34%, above Gen Z (#4). We do not have to teach
this audience to talk to an AI. They are already doing it, in ChatGPT, about their children, for free.
Every marketing dollar arguing "AI can help you parent" educates the market on ChatGPT's behalf.

**2. The adoption numbers contradict each other by 32 points and the gap is methodological.** Lurie says
81% of parents use AI for parenting tasks (#13); Common Sense says 49% of parents use AI often/sometimes
(#16); Pew says 49% of *all adults* have ever used a chatbot (#1). Lurie is Tier C — it discloses n and
month but no panel, no sampling frame, no margin of error, and it is a hospital marketing blog. Its 90%
"AI makes parenting easier" / 91% "boosts my confidence" figures are implausibly uniform and read like a
panel of AI-favourable respondents. **Plan against Common Sense's 49% and Pew's 61% of 30–49s, not against
81%.** Menlo's parent figures (#10, #12) are the most decision-useful because they were collected inside a
nationally-weighted general-population survey rather than a parenting-branded one.

**3. Logistics is already a mainstream AI use — for the general assistant.** Managing to-do lists (18%)
and meal planning (16%) are top-5 AI tasks among all US adults (#11); meal planning/grocery lists is the
#2 parenting AI use at 49% and activity planning is #5 at 37% (#14). Note what these have in common: they
are *one-shot generative* tasks. Nobody's evidence shows parents using general AI for the durable,
multi-week, multi-child state problems (registration windows, coverage gaps, who-drives-Thursday). That
is the actual seam, and it is narrower than "family logistics."

**4. Trust data is genuinely weak and the one strong study is unflattering to human experts, not to us.**
The 2024 Kansas study (n=116, blinded) found parents rated ChatGPT health text as *more* trustworthy than
expert text (#37). **I found no 2025–2026 US replication and no study that ranks parent trust in AI
against a pediatrician, teacher, friend, or influencer on a common scale.** That comparison the brief asked
for does not exist in citable form as of Aug 2026 — that absence is itself a finding. What we do have is
domain-specific hesitance (discipline 50%, health 38%, emotional 37% — #22) and near-universal institutional
distrust (64% not confident AI companies prioritize teen safety — #17). Read together: parents trust AI
*output* more than they admit, and AI *companies* far less than they trust the output. That argues for a
product that is unmistakably a tool for the parent, and against anything that looks like an AI that
relates to the child.

**5. The platform is eating this, on a published schedule, in the next 60 days.** Gemini Personal
Intelligence began free-tier rollout March 2026 (#29) and Google's own launch copy uses a *family trip
planning* example (#30). Google Assistant dies in September 2026 and Gemini inherits every Android phone
(#33). Apple ships Siri AI with personal context and mass App Intents in September 2026, free, on the
device (#32). The honest framing is not "will the platform eat this" — it is "the platform shipped the
first two courses in the last six months and the third lands the month after this research is filed."
What the platforms have *not* shipped is event-triggered watching (#41), shared multi-adult state, and
any model of a child as an entity with a schedule.

**6. Willingness to pay is the weakest link in the whole thesis.** Only ~3% of consumer AI users pay for
anything; ChatGPT itself converts ~5% of weekly actives (#26). Against that, AI apps convert *trials*
better than non-AI apps (8.5% vs 5.6%) and earn 41% more Year-1 LTV (#27) — so getting the first payment
is not the hard part. **The hard part is month 6:** AI apps retain 21.1% of payers at 12 months vs 30.7%
for non-AI, churn ~30% faster, and refund 20% more (#27, #28). RevenueCat explicitly notes this gap *opened
in 2026* — AI and non-AI retention were comparable in 2025. A $99–149/yr family AI product should be
modelled at AI-app retention (~21%), not parenting-app retention. **I found no Tier A/B/C data on
willingness to pay specifically for AI household/family help.** Anyone quoting such a number to us should
be asked for the origin.

**7. What has failed, and the pattern.** Beyond Milo (Jan 2026) and Yohana (US, Sept 2025) — both already
known — I could not source a single additional named US AI-household-assistant shutdown to Tier A/B/C.
Trade coverage references "a handful of smaller pure-play AI organizers" disappearing and Hearth Display
narrowing its AI ambitions, but only on vendor-run comparison blogs; that goes in WEAK SIGNAL below, not
here. The one *citable* pattern is structural rather than anecdotal: the RevenueCat 2026 retention cliff
(#27, #28) says AI consumer products in general — not family ones specifically — monetize early and
disintegrate late. Milo's own stated reason ("AI wasn't ready") is consistent with that. The failure mode
is not acquisition; it is that the assistant never accumulates enough state to be missed.

**8. What is working — and the honest answer is "nothing I can prove."** I found no Tier A/B/C evidence of
an AI consumer product with strong *retention* in the household/personal-assistant space. The retained
consumer AI products are the general assistants themselves (#7: ChatGPT at 44% of US adults) and the
non-AI incumbents (Cozi, Skylight — already known). The only differentiated thing the general assistants
do that we should copy is **passive memory accumulation**: ChatGPT builds a household profile by listening,
while our product charges ~34 taps of onboarding for the same asset. That is our worst competitive
liability and it is entirely self-inflicted.

**9. Implication for the kid-facing companion.** Every regulatory and attitudinal vector points the same
way: FTC 6(b) into seven companies over exactly this feature (#34), COPPA amended and enforced with real
penalties (#35, #36), 84% of parents worried about misuse of kids' data (#18), 75% wanting a federal
oversight body (#19), >80% of parents of young children worried about AI toys collecting data (#21), and a
majority explicitly not wanting AI to be their child's friend (#21). A kid-facing AI companion in a small
company's app in 2026 carries the compliance burden of a Meta feature with none of the legal budget. The
evidence supports cutting or heavily gating it.

## Section 2: WEAK SIGNAL

Not sourceable to Tier A/B/C. **Do not cite these as findings.**

- **"A handful of smaller pure-play AI family organizers quietly disappeared in 2025–26" and "Hearth
  Display narrowed its AI ambitions."** Appears only on `getsense.ai/blog` — a competing vendor's SEO

  worthless as evidence. (https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/best-ai-family-organizer-apps-2026)
- **"14,000+ AI startups launched in 2024, 3,800 shut down in 2025, 1,800 more in early 2026 = 40%
  failure rate."** A Medium post with no methodology and no traceable origin. This is stat laundering —
  the number has no publisher. **Do not use it.** (medium.com/@aiempiremedia)
- **"30% of parents of school-age children used ChatGPT in 2023."** Circulates via Futurism/blogs tracing
  back to a 2023 Axios/Ipsos-adjacent poll I could not resolve to a primary release with n and method.
  Superseded by #10/#16 regardless.
- **Lurie Children's 2026 figures generally (#13–15, #22–24, #38).** Included in Section 1 as Tier C
  because n and month are disclosed, but they lack a panel provider, sampling frame and MoE, and the
  publisher is a hospital marketing blog with an interest in the "parents rely on AI" narrative. Treat the
  *direction* as usable and the *magnitudes* as inflated.
- **ChatGPT scheduled-task limits (#41).** Sourced to a vendor-adjacent product blog. The claim is
  load-bearing for our "registration watchlist genuinely wins" verdict — **verify it in-product before
  putting it in a strategy doc or a pitch deck.**
- **A 2026 Menlo Ventures consumer AI report.** Does not appear to exist yet; the 3%-pay figure (#26) is
  from the June 2025 edition and is 14 months stale in a fast-moving market. Paid conversion has plausibly
  risen; we have no 2026 number.
- **Parent trust in AI vs. pediatrician / teacher / friend / parenting influencer on a common scale.**
  **No data found**, US or otherwise, 2025–2026. The BRIEF asked for it; it does not exist in citable
  form. Commissioning this question ourselves (n≈800 US parents, forced ranking) would be genuinely
  novel and cheap.
