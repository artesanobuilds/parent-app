# Addendum: Expanding the Wedge from "Camp Planning" to an Activity-Planning Portfolio

> **Note (2026-07-07):** the Activity OS frame in this addendum survived the adversarial review (`mvp-idea-roast.md`) intact and is incorporated into the canonical **`gtm-strategy.md`**, which adds three pillars on top of it: the registration-window watchlist, zero-friction second-parent participation, and the split data model (zero-retention coach).

## Five Verticals, One Job-to-be-Done — Research Expansion to the GTM Report

**Premise of this addendum.** The original report recommended camp planning as the acquisition wedge but flagged its biggest weakness: seasonality (Jan–Apr spike). Miguel's instinct is right — "camp planning" is one instance of a repeating parental job: *discover → decide → register → coordinate the things my kids do.* The deep-research agent, fit-scoring, flyer-vision ingestion, Gmail reading, and two-way calendar sync are all **activity-type-agnostic**. This addendum evaluates seven candidate verticals, selects five, and shows how they chain into a year-round "Activity OS" that solves the seasonality problem without diluting the wedge.

**Method note.** All market/spend claims below are sourced with year. Where a claim comes from a vendor or low-authority aggregator, it is flagged. Estimates are marked as estimates.

## 2. The Five Selected Verticals — Evidence Deep-Dive



### V1. Summer Camp + School-Break Coverage (the anchor, now broadened)

The original report covered summer camps; the expansion is **all school breaks**, which multiplies the episodes from one per year to four-plus.

- **Market/spend:** The US summer camps industry is ~$4.6B in 2025 revenue, growing at a 6.9% CAGR since 2020 (IBISWorld, Aug 2025). Note: global "children's summer camp" estimates conflict widely ($5.4B–$9.4B for 2025 across Global Growth Insights and Verified Market Research, 2025 reports) — the IBISWorld US figure is the most defensible anchor. American Camp Association data cited across sources: 12,000+ camps serving ~14M children annually.
- **Per-family spend:** 25% of parents planned to spend **over $2,000 per child** on summer camp in 2025, and 17% considered taking on debt to cover it (NerdWallet survey, cited 2025).
- **Pain intensity (the killer stat):** **85% of parents of kids under 18 report losing sleep over summer care and scheduling — 90% among working parents** (Bright Horizons 2026 Modern Family Index, Harris Poll, 2,000+ US adults, May 2026). 87% of working parents report challenges with kids home in summer; 68% say "summer feels like a break for everyone but themselves" (Bright Horizons, 2025–2026).
- **The break-coverage multiplier:** US public schools are closed an average of **~29 days per year beyond summer** — roughly double the vacation days a typical private-sector employee has (kidcentral TN, citing standard school-calendar data). 60% of working parents burn up to two weeks of PTO covering school/childcare closures (Bright Horizons survey, 2026). Parents report spending ~20% of household income on childcare against a federal "affordable" definition of 7% (Care.com 2026 Cost of Care Report).
- **Why this expansion matters for GTM:** winter break (book in Oct–Nov), spring break (book in Jan–Feb), and summer (research Jan–Apr) turn one campaign season into **three predictable acquisition spikes** — each a natural before/after content moment ("spring break is in 6 weeks and you have zero coverage → solved in one screen").



### V2. Year-Round Extracurriculars & Youth Sports Logistics (the highest-budget vertical)

This is the single largest proof-of-budget in the entire family-activity space, and it converts the product from seasonal to weekly.

- **Spend:** The average US sports family spent **$1,016 on their child's primary sport in 2024 — a 46% increase since 2019** — with additional sports adding ~$475, approaching ~$1,500/child/year (Aspen Institute Project Play National Youth Sports Parent Survey with Utah State and Louisiana Tech, n=1,848, fielded Nov–Dec 2024, published 2025). By sport: soccer $1,188, basketball $1,002, baseball $714, tackle football $581 (same survey).
- **Broader extracurriculars:** Parents with kids in extracurriculars spend an average of **$731/child/year**, and 71% of parents of minors have at least one kid in activities (86% among $100K+ households); 89% also plan summer-activity spending averaging $1,453 across kids ($2,123 for $100K+ households) (LendingTree/QuestionPro, n=2,000, 2023 — older survey, flagged).
- **Scale:** 55.4% of US kids 6–17 played organized sports in 2023 (~27.3M kids, National Survey of Children's Health via Project Play); the youth-sports industry is estimated at **$30–40B** — larger than the annual revenues of the NFL (Financial Times / Project Play State of Play, 2025). Nearly 20% of sports parents report going into debt for it (Project Play, 2025).
- **The logistics load:** many club-sport kids now play 3–5 days/week, 12 months/year (Project Play 2025) — practice schedules, game locations, carpool coordination, registration windows, gear deadlines. For a two-household family, every one of those items is a coordination event.
- **Enrollment seasonality:** fall-activity registration (Aug–Sep) and spring registration (Jan–Feb) are camp-planning-shaped moments, twice a year, with the weekly logistics grind in between. **This is the vertical that makes the product a daily habit rather than an annual tool.**
- **Whitespace:** the incumbent tools are provider-side (TeamSnap, SportsEngine, Sawyer, ActivityHero registration) — they organize *one activity's* communications. Nobody owns the **cross-activity, cross-kid, cross-household** view. That's exactly the fit-score + calendar + conflict-detection engine Miguel already built for camps.



### V3. Weekend & Local Family Outings ("what do we do Saturday?")

- **The job:** every week, tens of millions of parents run the same search — something age-appropriate, nearby, affordable, this weekend. For a co-parent, it's sharper: *"it's my weekend with the kids and I want it to count."* This is an emotionally loaded version of the query that generic AI answers generically.
- **Incumbents = content, not coordination:** Mommy Poppins ("millions of parents use our event calendar, camp, classes… guides," per its own marketing, 2025 — vendor claim, flagged), Macaroni KID and Hulafrog local newsletters, and local Facebook groups. All are directories/newsletters; none knows your kids' ages, interests, custody calendar, or what you did last weekend. None closes the loop into a plan on the calendar.
- **Spend:** low direct willingness-to-pay for "ideas" alone — this vertical monetizes indirectly (retention/engagement, and later local-activity affiliate). Its GTM value is **frequency**: a weekly-use surface produces weekly content ("Saturday sorted in 90 seconds") and weekly reasons to open the app between the big planning seasons.
- **Divorced-dad resonance:** this is the most authentic content vein for Persona A from the main report. "Dad-weekend ideas my 4-year-old and 10-year-old will BOTH like" is an underserved, highly shareable format.



### V4. School Life: Admin Ingestion + Special Events (the sleeper — highest product fit)

This vertical was not in the original brief but the evidence says it belongs in the top five, and Miguel has effectively already built the hard parts. It has two halves that share one ingestion pipeline: **(a) the ambient email/app stream** and **(b) discrete special events** — field trips, fundraisers, book fairs, spirit weeks, picture day, school carnivals — each of which arrives as a flyer or email and explodes into a multi-step task chain (sign the permission slip, pay the fee, note the date, pack the lunch, volunteer or not, which household is the kid in that morning?).

**(a) The ambient stream — quantified pain:**

- The average parent receives **80+ school/activity-related emails per month**; over half feel overwhelmed and **62% have missed an important event or deadline** buried in the inbox (Yahoo/Censuswide survey of 2,000+ parents, 2024, as cited by Leto). Parents juggle **10–15 school/activity apps** at once (Edsby survey, cited in Yahoo Lifestyle, Sept 2025).
- **The flyer is now digital — which makes it MORE machine-readable, not less.** Districts nationwide have replaced backpack paper with emailed flyer images: Peachjar, the dominant school e-flyer platform since 2013, claims access to **more than 25% of the nation's parents and guardians**, delivering district-approved flyers as full-color images straight to parent inboxes (Peachjar company materials, 2024–26 — vendor claim, flagged; corroborated by dozens of district adoption pages, e.g., Albuquerque, Puyallup, San Carlos). Critically, **Peachjar solves distribution for schools; it does nothing to organize the parent's side** — the flyer lands as an image and the extraction/scheduling/task work still falls on the parent. That image-in-inbox format is a perfect target for Miguel's existing vision-extraction pipeline, and paper flyers in backpacks (still common in non-Peachjar districts) are the photo-capture case he already built for camps.

**(b) Special events — frequency and proof of budget:**

- School events are not occasional: over 90% of schools have a PTO/PTA (Cheddar Up national school-fundraising survey, 2024–25), and common event types stack up across the year — book fairs (~~39% of schools; ~15% of annual school fundraising revenue), carnivals (~~42% of schools), fun runs averaging ~$15K raised per elementary school, plus product fundraisers dominating elementary revenue (aggregated fundraising statistics compilations, WifiTalents/Zipdo 2026 — **low-authority aggregators, treat directionally**, but consistent with National PTA and PTO Today ecosystem material).
- Parents *fund* these events at real scale: 75% of parents report willingness to participate in at least two school fundraisers per year (same aggregate compilations, flagged); and at the top of the market, parent organizations at the 34 most-affluent LAUSD elementary schools collected an average of **$876 per student per year**, with many booster clubs asking $1,000+/kid/yr (LAist analysis of 348 LAUSD parent-org filings, 2018-19 data, published 2022–25). Where there is that much event-driven money and volunteering, there is that much event-driven logistics.
- **The co-parent kicker:** every special event is a two-household coordination hazard — the permission slip goes home in one backpack to one house; the $15 fee, the costume for spirit day, and the early pickup land on whichever parent has custody that day. "Did you sign it? Did you pay it? Is it your morning?" is precisely the failure mode incumbents (ParentSquare, Peachjar, school apps) never see, because they treat "the family" as one inbox.

**Competitive picture:** Leto (Gmail → tasks/calendar/daily SMS, $12/yr, founder-built, 2025–26) and SchoolChatter (AI daily digests, "1,000+ parents," vendor claim) are single-feature email wrappers; ParentSquare and Peachjar own the school-side B2B channel, not the parent's life. **None is co-parent-aware and none connects school events to the rest of the family's activity calendar** (the soccer conflict with the field trip; the fundraiser dinner on a handoff night).

**Product fit:** Miguel's Gmail-reading agent and photo/flyer vision extraction ARE this feature — currently scoped to camps. Widening the ingestion filter from "camp emails" to "school + activity senders" (including Peachjar's [school@peachjar.com](mailto:school@peachjar.com) sender, a single well-known address) and teaching the extractor the event taxonomy (field trip / fundraiser / spirit day / picture day / early dismissal → typed task chains) is likely days of work, not months, and converts the product into **daily** utility during the 10 months camps aren't being planned. The demo is irresistible: *photo of the field-trip flyer → permission-slip task assigned to the right parent, fee to-do, calendar event on both households' calendars, packing reminder the night before.*

**Pricing signal (cautionary):** Leto at $12/yr suggests standalone school-email digestion commands low prices. As a *bundled capability* inside a $199/yr family chief-of-staff, it's a retention feature and a demo centerpiece, not the headline price-carrier.

### V5. Family Vacation & Break-Trip Planning (selected, but the weakest of the five — with caveats)

- **The trap to avoid:** generic AI trip planning is a red ocean of free/cheap tools — Layla (free, $49/yr premium, 2026), TripPlanner AI, Mindtrip, Wanderlog, plus ChatGPT itself, which parent media now actively recommends for family trips (The Everymom, updated Jan 2026). Competing on "AI plans your vacation" is the Milo mistake from the main report.
- **The defensible slice:** what none of those tools knows is (a) the custody calendar — *which weeks are even mine?*, (b) both kids' ages/temperaments/interests from the family profile, and (c) the rest of the family's activity commitments. For separated parents, vacation planning has a legal/coordination layer (travel consent between co-parents, alternating holiday schedules) that generic planners can't touch. **Position as "trip planning that already knows your custody schedule and your kids," never as "AI trip planner."**
- **Scope discipline:** itinerary depth, bookings, and flights should stay out of scope (link out); the product's job ends at *pick the week, pick the destination shape, put it on both households' calendars, generate the packing/consent to-dos.*

## 3. The Unifying Frame: One Agent, Five Activity Types, a Year-Round Relay

The strategic upgrade this expansion delivers is killing the original report's biggest stated risk (seasonality) without widening the ICP or the positioning. It's the same job-to-be-done on a rotating calendar:


| Months               | Acute episode                                                                   | Vertical   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Jan–Apr              | Summer-camp research & registration ("book by January or lose the spot")        | V1         |
| Jan–Feb              | Spring activity/sports registration + spring-break coverage                     | V2, V1     |
| May–Aug              | Summer logistics in motion (sessions, gear lists, camp emails) + vacation weeks | V1, V4, V5 |
| Aug–Sep              | Fall activity/sports enrollment + back-to-school email tsunami                  | V2, V4     |
| Oct–Nov              | Winter-break coverage scramble + holiday travel across households               | V1, V5     |
| Every week, all year | Practices, games, carpools, permission slips, "what do we do Saturday"          | V2, V4, V3 |


**Positioning implication:** the brand promise upgrades from "camp planner" to **"everything your kids do — found, decided, and on both calendars."** The wedge *entry point* stays seasonal and specific (whichever episode is next on the calendar at launch), but the retention story is now a 12-month loop instead of a 4-month spike. This materially strengthens the $199/yr price hypothesis from the main report: an annual subscription is much easier to justify against four planning seasons plus weekly utility than against one.

**Combined proof-of-budget (constructed estimate, order-of-magnitude):** a dual-activity family with two kids plausibly spends $2,000–4,000+/yr on camps (NerdWallet 2025) + ~$1,500–3,000/yr on sports/extracurriculars across kids (Aspen 2025; LendingTree 2023) + vacation spend. A $199/yr coordination layer is 3–5% of the activity budget it organizes — the "we already spend thousands on the activities; $199 to never drop one" framing writes itself.

## 4. What Changes in the Validation Funnel (Section 7 amendments)

1. **Quiz question 3 ("biggest friction right now?") expands its options** to map directly onto the five verticals: *summer camps & school breaks / weekly activities, sports & carpools / school stuff — emails, permission slips, field trips & fundraisers / weekends — finding things to do / trips & vacations / coordinating any of it with my co-parent.* The distribution of answers is now also a **wedge-priority vote** — Miguel gets the market to rank the five verticals for him inside the same 2-week, $20/day test.
2. **The Family Snapshot lead magnet gains one section:** "Your family's activity year" — a personalized 12-month map of the episodes above for *their* kids' ages (e.g., "your 10-year-old's camp registrations open in ~8 weeks; fall soccer signup for your district typically opens in August"). This is genuinely useful, unique to the family profile, and demos the agent.
3. **Ad-concept additions (hooks, same format as main report):**
  - "Your kid's whole year — camps, soccer, spring break, the school-email avalanche — on one screen both parents can see."
  - "80+ school emails a month. 62% of parents miss a deadline in there. Not anymore." (Yahoo/Censuswide 2024)
  - "It's your weekend with the kids. Here's a plan they'll both love — in 90 seconds."
  - "Fall registration opens in 3 weeks. Do you know which three things conflict? It does."
  - "Snap the field-trip flyer. Permission slip, $15 fee, and the date on BOTH parents' calendars — in 5 seconds." (the single best demo-format ad of the whole set: visual, before/after, and unfakeable)
  - "Spirit week, book fair, fun run, picture day — all in one backpack, all due Friday. Handled."
4. **Timing flexibility:** the original plan assumed launching into camp season. With five verticals, launch whenever ready and lead with **whichever episode is next on the calendar** (e.g., a July launch leads with fall-activity enrollment + back-to-school emails, not camps).
5. **Decision-gate addition:** if quiz responses show one non-camp vertical (most plausibly V2 or V4) winning the friction vote by >2x, re-order the marketing lead — the product doesn't change, the headline does.

## 5. Risks & Honest Counterpoints

- **Scope-creep optics.** Five verticals must be *one agent with five entry doors*, not five features on a homepage. The landing page should still lead with ONE episode (the next seasonal one) — the "activity year" map is the reveal, not the pitch. Donnelly's one-feature/one-client/one-problem rule still governs the *message*, even though the platform quietly covers more.
- **V5 (vacations) is the most ChatGPT-exposed** of the five; keep it custody-schedule-scoped or demote it to a content topic if the funnel shows no pull.
- **V3 (weekends) will not carry paid pricing alone** — treat it as engagement/retention and content, and resist building a local-events directory (that's Mommy Poppins' capital-intensive game).
- **V2 depends on data coverage** — camp discovery already works via web research; league/club registration windows are messier and more local. Validate the agent's hit-rate in 2–3 metro areas before promising it in ads.
- **Data gap flagged:** no reliable public benchmark exists for willingness-to-pay for *cross-activity coordination* specifically (as opposed to registration or advice). The quiz's WTP question, segmented by which friction the respondent selected, is how Miguel generates this data himself.

## So what this means for Miguel

- Generalize the deep-research agent's "activity type" parameter now — camps, classes/sports, break coverage, weekend outings, trips — it's the cheapest product change with the largest strategic payoff (seasonality solved).
- Widen the Gmail agent's filter from camp senders to school + activity senders (including Peachjar's single known sender address) and teach the extractor the school-event taxonomy — field trip, fundraiser, spirit day, picture day, early dismissal — each mapping to a typed task chain. V4 is nearly free to build and turns the app into a daily habit, and "did you sign the permission slip?" is a co-parenting wound none of the incumbents touch.
- The field-trip-flyer demo (photo → permission slip task + fee to-do + event on both households' calendars) should be filmed as the flagship before/after ad — it's the most visual, most verifiable proof of the whole platform in five seconds.
- Keep the *pitch* narrow (next seasonal episode) while the *platform* is broad; the "your family's activity year" map in the Family Snapshot is how you show the breadth without a feature-list pitch.
- Use the expanded quiz friction question as a free market vote on vertical priority — let 200+ respondents rank V1–V5 before you commit the headline.
- The $199/yr price now has a stronger anchor: it's ~3–5% of the $3,000–5,000+ a two-kid activity family already spends per year on the things this product coordinates.
